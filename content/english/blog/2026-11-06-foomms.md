***Note:*** *foomms relies on [foodjis](/blog/2026-09-24-foodjis-android/) and on a [PGP ID](/solutions/pgp-id/) identity. To keep up, [join our mailing lists](/contact/).*

**[foomms](https://foomms.foopgp.org/) is an SMS and MMS messaging app for
Android whose messages can only be read with their recipient's security
key.** It replaces the phone's messaging app, keeps its habits —
conversations, bubbles, a field at the bottom — and encrypts what goes out for
the person you are writing to, with your signature when you want it. It has
been on Google Play since mid-October, and can also be downloaded from its own
site.

Why SMS, in 2026? Because a phone number is the one address every phone
answers. No account to open, no app the other person must have joined, no
server of ours in between: the carriers transport the message like any other.
They see who writes to whom, and when. Not what is written.

## The number designates the key

A message *for a number* is encrypted *for the certificate that number
designates*. There is nothing to choose when writing, and nothing to mix up.

Few people put their mobile number in their certificate, and they should not
have to: a certificate is public. So the link between a number and a key is
kept where numbers already live, in the phone's address book. With your
consent, foomms writes there the certificate's address on a keyserver — the
form the `KEY` property of a vCard gives it
([RFC 6350, section 6.8.1](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6350#section-6.8.1)) —
and fills in the name, addresses and face where the contact does not say them
yet. The link is yours: each person decides for themselves which certificate
a number designates.

A signed message from a number not yet linked offers to link that number to
its signer's certificate. That is how the address book fills up, one
conversation at a time.

## No cryptography in foomms

foomms holds no key and does no cryptographic computation. It asks foodjis for
everything, through Android's OpenPGP provider interface, the one mail apps
already speak: foodjis keeps the keyring, encrypts, checks signatures, and
opens a message with the security key held against the back of the phone. The
private key never leaves the security key, and the phone holds nothing but
public material.

Two consequences, both deliberate:

- **foomms has no Internet access.** It does not ask Android for that
  permission: the phone's own service carries a message, and whatever is asked
  of a keyserver is asked through foodjis.
- **One touch of the key opens several messages.** Pressing an encrypted
  message opens with it the ones around it in the conversation — seven on each
  side by default, from none to fifteen — and a few unread messages from other
  conversations. foodjis says in its window each step of what it is doing; the
  phone vibrates when the key can go.

## What travels

**A text stays a text.** What goes out is a PGP message in its armor
([RFC 9580, section 6](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9580#section-6)):
characters an SMS carries without damage. Up to five SMS, the message goes as
SMS; beyond that, as a single MMS — or as soon as it takes more than one SMS,
if you prefer MMS. Whoever receives it without foomms sees a message that is
plainly encoded, and can copy it into foodjis.

**A photo, a sound, a video, a file** go as MMS. Encrypted or signed, the file
becomes a PGP message of its own, under its name followed by `.pgp`, which
foodjis opens like any file. A photo is scaled down before it is encrypted: a
carrier can no longer do it afterwards. An MMS stays under 300 KB, or under
your carrier's limit if you choose it.

**Four colours** say what each message is worth, without having to read a
word: red, neither encrypted nor certified; yellow, encrypted and not
certified; blue, certified and not encrypted; green, both. *Certified* means
signed by someone you have certified, or, for what you send, signed by you for
someone you have certified. A message not opened yet is grey: what it is worth
is only known once the key has been used.

## The plain text is never written

What an encrypted message said exists only in memory, for the time you choose
— five minutes by default, from thirty seconds to twelve hours —, then it turns
grey again. The phone's message store, the one every messaging app reads,
keeps only what travelled: the armor.

The same goes for everywhere foomms writes:

- **The backup** saves messages in the format most messaging apps read (that
  of "SMS Backup & Restore"), and PGP messages stay encrypted in it.
- **Drafts** stay in foomms, and are not lost when you switch to another app.
- **An encrypted message is also encrypted for you**, so that you can read it
  again, unless you set otherwise.

## Writing to several people, and the rest

**One message for several people**: named one after the other when writing,
each receives it in their own conversation. Encrypted, it is a single PGP
message for all their keys, like a letter with several recipients.

And also: a short video or a sound recorded in foomms, watched full screen; a
long voice message that keeps playing outside the conversation, its controls
in the notifications; a missing certificate looked up on the keyservers,
through foodjis; a text, an image or a file shared from another app, to one
or several people.

## What comes next

**The next version**, 1.3.3, is under review by Google. It brings together
everything done since 1.2.5:

- **MMS groups**, conversations among several people as in any messaging app.
  There is a pitfall, which we ran into for real: several carriers rewrite the
  "To" field of a group MMS, so that on arrival not everyone sees the same
  members. foomms therefore attaches to each message a small vCard,
  `foomms-group.vcf`, naming the members: the group is recognised by it,
  whatever the route. Encryption is optional there; a group can be named, on
  your phone only, and holds up to twenty members by default;
- **a multiple send**: the same message sent to each person in their own
  conversation, each copy encrypted for its recipient alone, and signed only
  once — one touch of the key for the whole send;
- **three pages**, *Conversations* — where one-to-one exchanges and groups
  follow one another —, *Unread* and *My profile*. At launch, foomms asks which
  address book contact is yours: it never reads the SIM card's number;
- **drafts kept where Android expects them**, so that another app sees them,
  but as a marker without text: what you were writing stays in foomms;
- **a text goes as SMS up to seven**, surer to arrive anywhere than an MMS;
- **a message the carrier refused says so** under its bubble: a refusal proves
  the message did not leave, while an acceptance does not prove it arrived;
- pixel faces, drawn from the certificate's fingerprint, for those without a
  photo; the frame in the certificate's colours in a conversation's title; a
  silent voice note, the microphone taken by a call, is not sent; after foodjis
  is updated, foomms reconnects on its own.

**The version after that**, 1.4.0, is ready, and waits for 1.3.3 to be
published:

- **the carrier's limits**, read from the configuration Android keeps for each
  network, are shown in the settings. You choose up to how many SMS a long text
  goes as SMS, from one to thirty-two, and how large an MMS may be, between
  300 KB and what the carrier announces. Some networks no longer carry MMS at
  all;
- **an encrypted attachment arrives whole.** We saw a carrier take out, on the
  way, a 316 KB attachment it judged too heavy for the receiving phone, and
  put a sentence of its own in its place. A photo in clear can be scaled down
  without much harm; an encrypted attachment scaled down is lost. An MMS can
  forbid it: with the field `X-Mms-Adaptation-Allowed: No` (OMA-MMS-ENC 1.3),
  a 574 KB encrypted attachment arrived whole. foomms sets it on every MMS
  that carries an encrypted or signed attachment, and should a carrier take
  one out all the same, the bubble says so;
- **what you send encrypted shows open** in your bubble, the photo as well as
  the text, for the same countdown;
- **received photos open full screen**: zoom in, move around, rotate;
- **two more shades**: a message certified, but signed by someone other than
  the person of the conversation — a forwarded message, for one — is violet,
  or yellow-green if it is also encrypted;
- **an MMS the phone could not download** is reported, and asked for again
  for seven days;
- **My profile** gathers drafts and unread messages, each on its own line,
  opening onto the list of the conversations concerned; a long press on a
  conversation, a group or one of its members opens its menu; what is
  encrypted stays open fifteen minutes by default.

**The leads** we will follow next:

- **sending your certificate** by MMS to whoever does not have it yet;
- **saying before writing** who can receive an encrypted message, from the
  certificates in the keyring that carry a number;
- **a number that changes hands**: carriers reassign numbers. A message
  encrypted for the former holder stays unreadable to the new one, but still
  reaches them; whoever gives up a number should revoke the identity of their
  certificate that carries it;
- **what carriers do with attachments**: some resize images, take some out,
  or relabel a type they do not know. That is why a PGP message is recognised
  by its bytes, never by its label; what was enough between two French
  carriers remains to be tested elsewhere, country by country.

## Download

foomms can be downloaded from **[foomms.foopgp.org](https://foomms.foopgp.org/)**,
from Google Play or directly: each version comes with its fingerprint and its
detached signature, made by a PGP ID identity. It needs foodjis, installed the
same way from [foodjis.foopgp.org](https://foodjis.foopgp.org/), and an NFC
security key.
